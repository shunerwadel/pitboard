### Intro

* *Suspension intro slide*  
* The goal of the suspension is to:  
  * Maintain a good aero “platform” while sacrificing as little “mechanical grip” (i.e. vertical load variation and lateral load transfer) as possible  
  * Provide the driver a balance and ride quality which allows them to operate the vehicle at the limit of the tires capability  
* Why do we have suspension at all?  
  * Consider a go-kart with no suspension  
    * Highly responsive on a very smooth surface  
    * Bumps, curbs, and surface transitions cause a big problem  
      * Tires come off the ground, terrible ride for driver  
      * We can’t have this at extremely high speeds  
  * Suspension allows us to overcome this problem  
    * Allows for influencing the behavior of the chassis/body (sprung mass) separately from the suspension near the wheel, or unsprung mass  
      * **Sprung mass** \- All mass supported by the suspension (chassis, engine, driver, body)  
      * **Unsprung mass** \- All mass that moves directly with the profile of the track (wheels, tires, hub, upright, brakes)  
    * Good suspension design allows the sprung mass to remain relatively still and stable while the unsprung mass moves rapidly to follow the profile of the road  
* Inboard vs outboard  
  * *Suspension inboard vs outboard slide*  
  * Typically talk about suspension as inboard (chassis side) or outboard (wheel side)  
  * Example: a coil over has an outboard mount on the unprung suspension, and an inboard mount on the sprung chassis
