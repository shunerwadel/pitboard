# Ackermann steering

In a low-speed turn, the inner front wheel must steer to a **tighter** angle than the outer wheel so both roll about the same instantaneous center.

## Ideal Ackermann

Lines drawn through the steering axes should meet at the center of the rear axle for the turn radius. Real race cars often run **less than** 100% Ackermann (or even reverse Ackermann) because high lateral slip angles dominate at speed.

## Teaching contrast

| Regime | What matters |
| --- | --- |
| Parking / hairpins | Geometric Ackermann reduces scrub |
| High-g corners | Tire slip curves and load matter more than pure geometry |

## Talk track

Show a plan-view sketch, mark inner vs outer steer angles, then ask when “perfect Ackermann” stops being the right target for an FSAE or club-race car.
