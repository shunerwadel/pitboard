### Forces & moment nomenclature

* *Aero force and moment slide*  
* Drag and thrust (Fx, longitudinal, CD)  
  * Force from air that acts in the opposite direction of the heading of the vehicle is called **drag**  
  * Where do we care most about drag?....in a straight-line  
    * Pushing air out of the way requires a lot of power   
    * Drag is the primary limiting factor for a race car's top speed, always want less drag  
  * Fx that acts in the same direction we are heading is:  
    * Good in a straight-line  
    * Can be upsetting to the car as it changes direction (like on corner entry or exit).  
* Side Force (Fy, lateral, CS)  
  * **Side force** \- Force from air which acts perpendicular to the car’s direction  
  * When do we generate side force?....when the vehicle goes into yaw (car is angled relative to the oncoming airflow)  
  * Side force can have a stabilizing effect so long as it is not due to wind  
* Lift and downforce (Fz, vertical, CL)  
  * When air flowing over, under or around the car generates downward force, we call it **downforce**   
  * We always want as much downforce as we can get  
    * In what scenario is lift a big concern?.....when a car spins around backwards (fly into air, roof flaps).  
  * The cost of downforce is drag  
    * **L over D (CL/CD)** \- Trade off between downforce and drag is a key concept in all forms of auto racing  
* Aero moments (Mx, My, Mz)  
  * When aerodynamic forces exerted by the air on the car are not “balanced” about the CofG, aero moments are created  
  * Pitch moment (My, CPM) \- difference in Fz (downforce) on the front and rear axle. Largest impact on balance.  
  * Roll moment (Mx, CRM) \- difference in Fz (downforce) between the left and right sides. Not super concerned about roll moment (cars are generally symmetrical).  
  * Yaw moment (My, CYM) \- difference in Fy (side force) on the front and rear axles. This impacts stability and promotes yawing behavior.  
* Quantifying aerodynamic behavior  
  * **Count** \- We typically quantify aerodynamic forces using dimensionless coefficients called counts (CD, CL, CLF, CLR, CS, CPM, CRM, CYM).  
    * Drag counts  
      * F1: 1.5  
      * NASCAR Cup: 0.4  
      * IndyCar: 1.4  
    * Downforce counts  
      * F1: \-6.0  
      * NASCAR Cup: \-1.0  
      * IndyCar: \-4.5  
  * Allows us to compare aerodynamic efficiency of different shapes and vehicles regardless of the car's speed or size.
